Landscaping SEO that fills the calendar before the season

Landscaping demand follows the calendar and the weather. Homeowners start planning weeks before the first mow or the first planting. Landscaping SEO is about having the right pages up before that curiosity turns into calls.

Request a site reviewAll industries

Typical landscaping site issues

  • Photos without context. Galleries show pretty images but never say where, what or why.
  • Mixed services. Lawn care, design, patios and tree work crowd one page.
  • Late seasonal content. Spring cleanup pages are published after the demand peak has passed.
  • Unclear service zone. Visitors cannot tell whether the company works in their neighborhood.

What we fix first

  • Distinct pages for maintenance, design, hardscape, planting and any specialty services.
  • Project pages naming the town, scope and materials, with captions that help search and people.
  • Season-ready pages scheduled to publish ahead of demand.
  • A service area map or list tied to town pages.
  • Profile photos and posts matched to current jobs.

Photos are your best asset

Landscaping is visual, so image handling matters. Oversized photos slow pages on phones, and unlabeled ones waste a chance to describe the work.

We compress images, name the files sensibly and write captions that give search engines the context they cannot see.

Where to start

Work back from your busiest month. Pages for that service should be live well ahead of it, with project photos and the profile already in place. A review marks which seasonal pages exist, which are stale and which are missing, so the work follows the calendar rather than chasing it.

Questions from landscaping companies

When should I publish seasonal pages?

Several weeks before search interest rises. That gives search engines time to crawl and rank them.

How do I make a gallery useful for SEO?

Add a short caption stating the town, the work done and the materials used, and group images by project.

Is SEO worth it for a small crew?

Often yes, if you focus on the few services that fill your schedule and avoid spreading thin.
