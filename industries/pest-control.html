Pest control SEO for people who found something crawling

Pest searches are specific. People type the name of what they found, sometimes a photo description. Pest control SEO starts from that: a page for each species you treat, written to answer the question behind the search.

Request a site reviewAll industries

Why pest sites underperform

  • One catch-all page. Ants, rodents, wasps and termites are crammed together, so the site matches few exact searches.
  • Seasonal blindness. Mosquito and wasp pages appear after the surge has started instead of before.
  • Vague treatment descriptions. Visitors cannot tell what a visit involves or whether it suits a home with pets.
  • Weak local signals. Service towns are listed in a footer paragraph rather than described on pages.

What we fix first

  • A page for each major pest, with identification help, signs of infestation and treatment overview.
  • A seasonal calendar so relevant pages are updated ahead of peaks.
  • Plain explanations of safety questions, without absolute promises.
  • Area pages tied to the pests common in each region.
  • Profile services and categories matched to the species list on the site.

Identification pages do double duty

A page that helps someone work out what they are looking at attracts the searcher who does not yet know the pest name. It can also earn quotes in AI answers, which often start with identification.

We add a short, clear call to action at the point where the reader realises they need help.

Where to start

Pick the five pests that pay best and build those pages first, with the season calendar beside them. The long tail of rarer pests and the town pages follow. A review compares your pest coverage with the leading local rivals for each species.

Questions from pest control operators

How many pest pages do I need?

One for every pest you actively treat and want calls about. Rare ones can be grouped in a guide.

Do AI assistants answer pest questions?

Yes, often with general advice. Pages that explain identification and next steps clearly are more likely to be cited.

Can I mention safety?

Yes, factually and carefully. Avoid absolute claims, and describe what the technician will do.
